The single next experiment

Tick-resolution test of the 15:30 server (8:30 New York) scheduled-release straddle, Thursdays and Fridays first.

It is the only thing in 222 ledger entries that is net positive in development, validation and out of sample after costs, and the only one with a mechanism you can name: the weekly US data releases at 8:30 New York (initial jobless claims every Thursday, payrolls and CPI on their Fridays). The five-minute bar ending 15:30 server predicts a 1.75x larger range over the next 100 minutes (AUC 0.80, stable in every period), and a straddle with stops 0.25 ATR either side and a 0.75 ATR target earns +0.12, +0.29 and +0.21 ATR per event in the three periods, +0.54 on Thursdays, positive in each of the six years, while the same straddle at random bars loses 0.15 to 0.41 ATR.

It is also undecidable with the data we have. In 86% of these events the first minute after 15:30 is wider than both triggers, and 390 of 827 events could not be resolved at one-minute resolution. Count those as losses and the expectancy is about -0.21 ATR; count them as wins and it is about +0.45. Only tick data says which side triggered first and what the spread did. Dukascopy publishes gold ticks with bid, ask and both sizes for free; the experiment needs roughly 120 release days (every Thursday of 2025 plus the payroll and CPI days), designed on 2025 and held out on 2026, a fixed configuration with a plateau check from 0.15 to 0.40 ATR, and Dukascopy's own spread plus XM's 51 points as the cost. If it survives, the follow-on is a four-week XM demo run placing the OCO at 15:29:30 with fill logging, which is the execution-edge question in your section 9. Zero data cost, about two hours of work, one predefined hypothesis.

  • Timezone and DST are consistent. Monday's first bar is 01:00 server in 155 of 167 weeks and Friday's last bar 23:00 in 148 of 168, which is XM's EET schedule; the 12 exceptions are holiday weeks. Report B: Manual edge experiment

Exact fields and how they will be tested.

  • Per trade, at entry (before the outcome is known): timestamp to the second, symbol, direction, entry price, initial stop, target if any, lot; TWK signal on the chart at that moment (yes/no, which timeframe, minutes since the flip); one line for why entered; expected type (continuation or reversal); whether you waited for the candle close, a pullback, or a confirmation; the higher timeframes you looked at and what they showed; news you were aware of; a screenshot.
  • Per trade, at exit: exit time and price, reason (stop, target, discretionary, opposite signal, time), result in R, and whether you moved the stop and why.
  • Per skipped signal: every TWK flip you saw and did not take, with the time and one line for why. This is the half of the dataset that matters most; without it the comparison in section 2 of your brief cannot be made.
  • Minimum: 100 trades and all the skipped signals in the same weeks; a second 100 later as the out-of-sample set.

Tests

  • Join every manual trade and every skipped signal to the signal dataset by time (the 73-feature table already exists), so A/B/C/D are exact sets: manual with TWK, manual without, TWK skipped, TWK accepted.
  • For each set: expectancy, PF, win rate, average R, MFE and MAE in R, P(+0.5R), P(+1R), P(+2R), MAE before +1R, time to +0.5R and +1R, duration, measured with the same first-passage engine as the bot's signals, so the comparison is like for like.
  • The decisive number: expectancy of the signals you took versus the signals you skipped, in the same weeks. If skipped signals are not worse than taken ones, selection has no information and the manual edge, if any, is in exits or sizing.
  • Decision variables: your written reasons coded as binary features (waited for close, pullback, HTF up, near level, news day, and so on), each tested for correlation with the outcome across all trades, winners and losers alike; then Models A to D (TWK only, price context, manual variables, both) fitted on the first 100 and scored on the second 100 by expectancy, PF, top-decile precision and +1R / +2R hit rates, not AUC alone.
  • Rejection rule: a manual variable that does not improve out-of-sample expectancy is a story, not a rule.

At hourly resolution the cross-asset information is already in gold's own price by the time the bar closes: rank correlations between an asset's hourly return and gold's next one to sixty hours are below 0.03 in every period and change sign between periods; after a 2-sigma hour in silver, gold's next hour averages +0.03 ATR whether silver rose or fell; the monetisation rows are within noise of zero. If a cross-asset lead exists it is shorter than an hour, which makes it a tick-data question (Report E), not a bar question.

Full design (what the data here could not cover)

  • Series: silver, platinum, EURUSD, USDJPY, S&P 500, Nasdaq, oil, bitcoin at H1 for five years (XM, cheap on disk one at a time), and a continuous dollar index and 10-year yield from an external daily or hourly source. XM's dollar index and VIX are single futures contracts and cannot be used for history.
  • Tests: lead/lag at 1, 3, 5, 10, 15, 30, 60 bars; shock response by direction and magnitude; divergence (gold and dollar both up, gold and silver diverging, gold and yields diverging over the last three bars) with the next-k gold move; regime-conditional shocks (high versus low gold ATR ratio); each by DEV / VAL / OOS with the same acceptance rule as the ledger.
  • Tick-level version: the same lead/lag at 1 to 60 seconds between silver and gold ticks, and between EURUSD and gold around 15:30 releases, from Dukascopy. This is the version the hourly data cannot rule out.

Report D: Macro and news experiment

  • Fields per event: release timestamp (UTC to the second), country, event name, importance, previous, forecast, actual, revision; from a free calendar export (several sites export CSV; the FOMC statement and press-conference times separately).
  • Join: to server time (EET) minute bars now, to ticks later. Tag every bar within +/- 60 minutes of a release with the event and its surprise (actual minus forecast, standardised by the event's own history).
  • Scope: US CPI, PPI, payrolls, jobless claims, retail sales, GDP, PCE, ISM, FOMC decisions and minutes, Fed speeches; ECB and BoE decisions as a control group.

Tests

  • Pre-event: range, spread and the sign of the last 30 minutes' move by event class (positioning), versus the same clock on non-release days.
  • Reaction: at 1, 3, 5, 15 minutes from bars (1, 5, 10, 30 seconds need ticks): does the sign of the surprise predict the sign of the move (hit rate above 50% by event class, DEV / VAL / OOS), and does the size of the surprise predict the size?
  • Post-event: continuation versus reversal of the first-minute move over 15, 30, 60 minutes; range expansion versus the unconditional profile; whether the straddle result in Report F is a release-day effect only (the weekday split says yes: Thursday +0.54 ATR, Monday to Wednesday +0.06 to +0.08).
  • Acceptance as in the ledger, per event class, with the surprise sign as the directional hypothesis and the range as the volatility hypothesis, tested separately.

The 15:30 server effect measured in Report F is this experiment done blind: the clock knows when 8:30 New York is, the calendar would tell it which days matter. That is why the calendar is bundled into the next experiment rather than run alone.

Report E: Tick and microstructure experiment

  • Source: Dukascopy tick data for XAUUSD (and silver, EURUSD for the cross-asset version): timestamp in milliseconds, bid, ask, bid size, ask size. Free; about 150 MB per month as CSV; three months first, then the 15:30 windows over two years.
  • Derived per second and per 5 seconds: spread, spread relative to its 10-minute median, tick arrival rate, signed tick imbalance (upticks minus downticks), top-of-book size imbalance, micro-price (size-weighted mid) minus mid, consecutive same-side pressure, spread-normalised displacement, liquidity withdrawal (size falling while spread widens).

Tests

  • Information: for each feature, P(+0.1R / +0.25R / +0.5R before the mirror) over the next 1 to 5 minutes, with R = the 5-minute ATR, by DEV / VAL / OOS inside the sample, same acceptance rule. Horizons this short have never been tested here.
  • Execution: the true bid/ask path through the first minute after 15:30 (which trigger fired first, how far the ask ran, how wide the spread went), and at all hours: how often a stop order would fill inside the quoted spread versus beyond it. This decides the straddle and quantifies every stop-based result in the earlier reports.
  • Timing: whether entering after spread normalisation (spread back under its median within N seconds) changes the expectancy of the same events, which is the execution-edge test separated from the information-edge test.

Direction was a coin flip for every event; range is not. The table shows which events raise the next-100-minute range and by how much, with the order preserved in all three periods for the top eight. The time-of-day profile is the deterministic part: the two hours before and after the US 8:30 releases carry double the range of the New York afternoon.

Monetising range without direction means a straddle, and a straddle at random bars loses 0.26 to 0.37 ATR per attempt because two spreads and one stop are paid to catch a move that usually does not come. After the 15:30 bar the tightest straddle is positive in all three periods (+0.12, +0.29, +0.21 ATR), on Thursdays +0.54, in every year from 2021 to 2026; the shock-bar straddle is positive in all three periods as well (+0.02, +0.12, +0.20) and the climax-bar straddle in two. Wider straddles (0.5 and 1 ATR triggers) lose, so at bar resolution the effect has no plateau, which is a warning; for a release straddle the tight trigger is the design, but it is also the configuration most exposed to fills.

The unresolved half is the whole question. 86% of these events have a first minute wider than both triggers; the 390 unresolvable events would be losses of 0.5 ATR plus spread if the second trigger fires after the first, and wins if the first side runs. Between -0.21 and +0.45 ATR per event, and the bar data cannot say which. Status in the ledger: WEAK, execution-dependent, the next experiment.
