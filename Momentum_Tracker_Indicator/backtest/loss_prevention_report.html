Bottom line

The permission system does what it is for: when the signal is wrong it loses much less, and it does not get in the way when the signal is right. Over five years on M3 the baseline lost $2,752 at 0.02 lot on 3,399 trades. The recommended stack loses $572 on 835 trades, the loss falls in every period (development -$1,579 to -$373, validation -$705 to -$198, out of sample -$467 to -$1), the longest losing streak drops from 19 to 16, and the drawdown from $2,856 to $907. With 0.5% risk sizing the same five years end at $857 from $1,000 with a 15.8% maximum drawdown, against a wipe-out at fixed lots.

It does not make money, and nothing here suggests it can. Every phase and every period stays negative or flat. Among the 2,632 trades the stack blocks, 1,599 would have lost and 1,031 would have won: the gates are filtering a coin flip whose losses are larger than its wins, which is why blocking pays. The net benefit of blocking ($2,174) is almost exactly the improvement; the management changes add $97.

Enable Level 1 and the four Level 2 and 4 items that helped in all three periods. Keep the volatility ban, the chop lockout, the hybrid stop and the health exit switched off: each looked good on 2021 to 2024 and hurt in 2025 to 2026.

The accounting separates three effects. Blocking: baseline trades the protected run did not take, valued at what they actually did. New trades: signals the protected run took because a blocked trade had freed the slot. Management: the difference on trades both runs took. For the recommended stack the improvement is $2,179, of which $2,174 is blocking, $97 management, and -$92 the new trades let in.

Blocked trades are 61% losers by count and lose more per trade than the blocked winners make, so blocking a random sample of this signal is profitable. The gates differ a lot in precision. The 2-bar confirmation blocks 116 trades of which 106 would have lost (+$854 net benefit): a flip that reverses within two bars is the one thing this signal does reliably predict. The 2 ATR distance blocks 1,325 (56% losers, +$596), the large-candle lock 271 (56%, +$300), the spread gates 542 (73%, +$424). The daily-loss limit and the stop-for-the-day rule block 299 trades that are exactly 50/50 (-$240 together): they are damage limiters, not trade selectors, and their value is the survival shown in section 7, not the P&L here.

8. What to enable

Three levels: hard safety, loss reduction, experimental.

  • Level 1, always on: risk sizing 0.5% with the minimum-lot skip, daily loss 2%, streak ladder (2 half, 3 pause, 4 stop), spread / ATR <= 0.5, spread / (2R) <= 0.10, margin check, max exposure. Measured: -$2,752 to -$1,551 alone, ruin to -73% with sizing.
  • Level 2, retained on evidence: 2 ATR distance from the previous signal (+$1,596, all periods), 2-bar direction confirmation (+$610; do not use 1 bar), 2 attempts per direction with a pivot-and-1-ATR reset (+$118), and the R locks at +1R and +1.5R (+$626, positive out of sample). Together with Level 1: -$572, -$14% on equity.
  • Level 3, experimental, off by default: volatility ban (hurts out of sample; use the half-risk rule instead), chop lockout (no effect), hybrid stop (mixed), trade-health exit (negative), HTF and session filters (rejected in phase 1), news window (no data), execution-quality gate (no data). The large-candle lock is the exception: positive alone (+$353) and inside the stack (+$300), so it is included in the recommended set.
  • The result to expect: a system that trades about 170 times a year instead of 700, loses slowly instead of quickly, and survives. Not a system that makes money. That would need a signal with an edge, which phase 2 showed this one is not.

10. Untested, and the telemetry that would test it

  • News windows. No economic-calendar data offline. The engine has no event gate; the MQL5 inputs NewsMinutesBefore / NewsMinutesAfter and a per-trade near_event flag should be added so a demo run can measure it.
  • Spread spikes. The spread model is a yearly median, so SpreadSpikeMultiple could not be evaluated; the EA's existing auto limit (2x the median of the last 1,000 bars) stays on and should log spread_at_signal, spread_at_order, spread_at_exit.
  • Execution quality. No fill data. Log signal_price, requested_price, fill_price, slippage, latency_ms, atr_at_signal; reject when expected slippage exceeds a configurable share of the stop.
  • Tick-level trailing. Bar-level replay; the phase-1 worst-path variant shows the direction of the error (worse), not its size.
  • Decision log. Every signal, every tag, every counterfactual: the CSV that the engine now writes (decisions) is the format the EA should write too, with the run id, so the same attribution table can be built from live demo data.
