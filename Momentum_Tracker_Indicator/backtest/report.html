Verdict

Neither TWK bot has an edge, on any timeframe, in any year. Both are one indicator: a Supertrend with factor 1.5 and ATR length 10. On gold it flipped 85,968 times in five years of one-minute bars, about 70 times a day. Every flip is a trade for the Pine bot, and the Momentum bot's volume, box and ADX filters are computed from the same price bars, so they thin the trades without changing their sign.

Before costs the system is a coin flip (gross P&L within a few hundred dollars of zero on every timeframe). After XM's spread it is a steady loss: the Pine bot on M1 paid $63,498 in spread to lose $66,887 at 0.02 lot. The best fix candidate, trading only 15:00 to 20:00 server time, still loses on every timeframe. The 95% confidence interval of the average trade is below zero for every base run, so this is not bad luck.

Do not put real money on either bot. At $200 and 0.02 lot every configuration except Momentum M15 wipes the account inside the last six months, which matches what the Strategy Tester did to you on 25 September (balance $7.02 by May 2026).

Why they lose

The mechanics behind the numbers.

  • The signal is noise at this frequency. Supertrend(1.5, 10) on a one-minute gold chart flips 70 times a day. Half of all Pine-bot trades end on the purple line within a few minutes; 12% are closed by the next flip; only 8% ever reach the 1:2 target.
  • Spread is the whole loss on M1. Pine M1: $66,887 lost, $63,498 of it spread. Momentum M1: $5,264 lost, $5,222 spread. Before costs both are flat. A strategy that only loses its transaction costs has no information in it.
  • The filters are made from the same bars. Buying and selling volume in the Tracker is tick volume split by whether the bar closed up or down, so a LONG flip after green bars nearly always passes the M1 and M3 boxes. ADX above 20 is true after most flips because a flip is a move. Removing every filter (the nofilters row) gives 5 times more trades and 6 times more loss; the filters are a volume knob, not an edge.
  • The initial stop is structurally wrong. 62% of Momentum M1 trades are quick re-flips (a LONG within 15 minutes of a SHORT). Those take the hard 500-point stop and lost $3,495 of the $5,264 total. The other 38% use a 5-bar pivot that can sit $30 to $80 away on today's gold.
  • The trailing gives back what the trend gives. On M15 the Momentum bot's exits are 45% by the 1:1 trail and 24% by the purple line versus 6% at the target. It is designed to take small pieces of trends and full-size losses on the stop. Tick-level trailing is harsher than my bar-level replay can show: under the worst-path assumption (the worstpath rows) the M1 loss grows from $5,264 to $7,580.
  • Position size guarantees ruin. Fixed 0.02 lot with pivot stops means single trades risking 10% to 100% of a $200 account. Every base run except Momentum M15 hits zero from $200 in the last six months alone.

The session filter (entries 15:00 to 20:00 server time, the London and New York overlap) is the only change that helps every bot on every timeframe, and it still loses: Momentum M1 -$642, M5 -$524, M15 -$298 over five years. Raising the volume ratio back to the default 1.5 halves the trades and the loss on M1 but changes nothing on M15. Reversing on the opposite signal, the setting you used in the 25 September tester run, is the worst variant on every timeframe. The pure Pine strategy (enter only when flat, no trailing) is better than the reverse-on-flip bot but still loses $12,265 on M1.

What would actually be needed is not in the parameter space: a stop capped at a multiple of ATR (skip the trade if the pivot is wider), a trail measured in ATR instead of points, one trade per direction per session, and risk-percent sizing with a hard cap on the stop distance. Even with all of that, the flip itself has to carry an edge, and over five years it does not.

To answer "make me profitable scenarios" honestly I searched 144 settings around yours (volume ratio 1.2 / 1.5 / 2.0, ADX 20 / 25 / 30, target 1:1 / 1:2 / 1:3 / none, all hours or 15:00 to 20:00, hold or close on flip), tuned on 2021 to 2024 and then checked on 2025 to 2026 without changing anything. Four settings were profitable in the tuning period, by at most $12 on 50 trades. Thirty-eight were profitable in 2025 to 2026. Not one is profitable in both. That pattern is what a strategy with no edge looks like when the market regime changes: whatever fits the calm 2021 to 2024 years loses in the trending 2025 to 2026 years and the other way round.

The best 2025 to 2026 setting (ratio 1.5, ADX 20, target 1:1, entries 15:00 to 20:00) made $403 on 264 trades with a profit factor of 1.24 and $131 in the last six months, but lost $387 with a profit factor of 0.61 on the four years before, and even in its good period the 95% interval of its average trade runs from -$0.47 to +$3.70. If you forward-test anything from this family on demo, test that one, size it at 0.01 lot on at least $1,000, and expect it to stop working when volatility falls. Do not trade it for real on this evidence.

Method, validation and limits

What the numbers can and cannot tell you.

  • Data. Dukascopy XAUUSD one-minute bid candles, UTC shifted to XM server time (EET), prices rounded to XM's 0.01 point. XM's own bars were exported too (M1 back to June 2026, M15 back to July 2022) and used for the spread model: 32 points in 2021 to 2023, 36 in 2024, 38 in 2025, 51 in 2026, which are XM's medians. Swap is XM's: long -86.84 and short +19.79 points per night, triple on Wednesday.
  • Engine. An exact port of TWK_Core.mqh (Supertrend, 5-bar pivots, up/down volume rows, ADX) drives both bots with their shipped preset values: Momentum ratio 1.2, hard SL 500 points on M1, re-flip within 15 minutes, trail 200/500/100, one position per symbol; Pine reverse on flip, purple trail from the first profitable tick, lock +900 at +1000. Management runs on one-minute bars. Inside a bar a stop is checked before a target, and a trailed stop is tested against the bar's extreme when the bar closes against the trade.
