Bottom line

The automated strategy has no edge after costs, and no filter set creates one. On M3 the raw signal plus the shipped management makes about $0.80 gross per trade at 0.02 lot and pays $1.56 of spread per trade. Every filter that passes the retention rule shrinks the loss by trading less; none turns the profit factor above 1.0 in the development or validation years, and the walk-forward test years are negative in all three windows (profit factor 0.53, 0.77, 0.99).

The loss mechanisms you suspected are real and measurable, and this report ranks them. But 87% of the loss is initial-stop hits on trades whose best point was 0.15R: the entry is wrong far more often than the exit. Filters that remove those trades remove nearly as many winners, because the winners look the same at the moment of entry.

Do not automate this signal for real money. What is worth doing: run the two-filter M3 variant (signal age and distance) on demo with risk-based sizing to collect the telemetry in section 13, and answer the questionnaire in section 15 so the discretionary part of your manual trading can be tested as rules.

2. Exact loss mechanisms

Ranked by how much of the loss they explain. Evidence for each is in section 3.

  • 1. Spread larger than the gross edge. Baseline M3, 3,399 trades: gross before costs +$2,710, spread $5,318, net -$2,752. On M1 the median spread was 13% to 18% of the target distance in 2021 to 2024; on M3 7% to 10%; on M15 3% to 4%. Table 3.1.
  • 2. Entries that never move. 1,686 initial-stop exits carry 87% of the gross loss. Their median best excursion is 0.15R: the market went against the signal almost immediately. Table 3.2 and 3.4.
  • 3. Volatility spikes. Losers entered when ATR was above 1.5x its 30-day median are 27% of losers but 41% of the loss (average -$13 against -$8.6). Capping them helps in the range years and hurts in the trend years (A/B table, atr_ratio<=1.5).
  • 4. Rapid re-flips. 524 losers came within 5 bars of the opposite signal, 25% of the loss. Requiring the opposite signal to be at least 10 bars old is the single most useful filter (PF 0.60 to 0.66 in DEV, 0.58 to 0.74 in VAL), and it is still below 1.
  • 5. Session and time-of-day. The three worst development hours carry 19% of the loss, but the hourly sign agrees between periods only 62% to 75% of the time (correlation 0.2 to 0.4). Not stable enough to trade on.
  • 6. Trailing that gives back. 566 losers were up 0.5R or more first (15% of the loss). Of trades reaching +1R, 13% still ended as losers. An R-based lock at +1R converts some of them but costs as many 2R targets; the net effect is about zero.
  • 7. Post-loss re-entry: an M1 problem, not an M3 problem. On M3 trades after a loss have better expectancy (-$0.60 to -$0.79) than trades after a win (-$0.98). Cooldowns change almost nothing here; they mattered on M1 where 55% of the loss came from trades closed within 3 minutes.
  • 8. Fixed lots. Not a source of the negative expectancy, but the reason a negative expectancy becomes ruin: fixed 0.02 lot from $1,000 ends at -$1,752 on the baseline (max drawdown 281%); 0.5% risk per trade on the same trades ends at $327, a 67% loss with no margin call. Sizing sets the speed of the loss, not its sign. Section 7.

4. Proposed filters

Only what the data supports. Each keeps its parameters configurable; none is a fixed threshold.

  • Signal age (retained). Problem: rapid re-flips (mechanism 4). Rule: skip the signal if the previous opposite signal is fewer than MinOppositeSignalBars bars old (10 on M3). Effect: DEV PF 0.60 to 0.66, VAL 0.58 to 0.74, trades -44%. Risk: it removes the first leg of every V-shaped reversal; in a fast trend day it removes nothing. It is the only filter whose improvement replicates in all three periods, and it is still negative.
  • Distance from the previous signal (retained). Problem: chop inside one price zone. Rule: skip if |close - previous signal close| < MinSignalDistanceATR x ATR (2.0). Effect on top of signal age: DEV loss -$673 to -$133, VAL -$213 to -$15, but trades fall to 268 / 80 / 390. Risk: over-filtering; the two together remove 78% of trades.
  • ATR-capped hybrid stop (retained, small). Problem: uncapped pivot stops ($58 at the 90th percentile in 2026). Rule: SL distance = max(structure, StopATRFloor x ATR), skip if > StopATRCap x ATR (1.0 and 4.0). Effect: PF 0.60 to 0.65 DEV, 0.58 to 0.66 VAL; removes the account-sized single losses. Risk: none material; this one is about survival, not edge.
  • Risk-based sizing (required). Not a filter. Lot = equity x RiskPercent / (stop distance x tick value per lot), broker min/step/max from SymbolInfoDouble, skip if 0.01 lot would risk more than MaxStopRiskPercent, pause after MaxDailyLossPercent or MaxConsecutiveLosses. Effect: converts a wipe-out into a drawdown (section 7: baseline -275% at fixed 0.02 lot versus -67% at 0.5% risk). Risk: with a $200 account most signals are skipped because 0.01 lot already exceeds the risk cap; that is the correct behaviour.
  • Cost gate (recommended as a guard, not as a filter). Rule: reject if spread / (RR x stop) > MaxCostToReward (0.10) or spread / stop > MaxCostToStop (0.20). Effect on M3: neutral (it removes the small-stop trades, which lose and win alike). Its value is on M1, where it rejects three quarters of signals and states the real reason M1 cannot work. Keep it as a safety rule with logging.
  • Spread-spike guard (recommended, untestable here). Rule: reject if current spread > SpreadSpikeMultiple x median spread of the last SpreadWindowBars bars (already in the EA as the auto spread limit). Needs tick spread history to evaluate; see section 14.

Rejected filters and why

  • Post-loss cooldowns (all 26 variants). On M3, trades after a loss are not worse than other trades; cooldowns move the result by single dollars. Would matter on M1, but M1 fails the cost gate anyway.
  • Higher-timeframe alignment (M15, H1, EMA slope). Sign flips between the range and trend regimes; the OOS gains are hindsight.
  • Session filters. Hourly expectancy does not replicate between periods.
  • Pure ATR stops (1 to 3 ATR). Tighter stops produce more stops and the same spread on each; PF falls in DEV at 1.0 and 1.5 ATR.
  • Trail activation at 0.25R to 0.75R and break-even at 0.5R to 1R. All worse: normal noise hits the moved stop before the trend starts (winners' median MAE is 0.37R, and 36% of winners go beyond 0.5R against before winning).
  • Time exits. Freeing the slot early lets the next losing signal in; post-hoc saving under 5% of the loss.
  • Opposite-signal models A, B, D. Closing on the opposite signal is worse than holding in every form; Model B (confirm 2 bars) is the least bad and still below the baseline. Your tester setting (reverse) is the worst of all.
  • Chop filters (flip count, Bollinger width, opposite candles, ADX slope). Either remove almost nothing on M3 (flip count, Bollinger width) or remove winners and losers alike.
  • Entry-quality score. Marginal and six-parameter; two of its features reverse out of sample.
  • Volatility caps (ATR ratio <= 1.5). Helps VAL, hurts OOS badly (PF 0.89): the 2025 to 2026 winners were the high-ATR trades.

The exit design is the part that works: 1:1 trail exits and TPs are the only positive exit types in every period. The lab did not find a management change that beats the shipped one by more than noise; it found several that are worse.

14. Missing data

Things this study could not test honestly.

  • Economic calendar. No CPI/NFP/FOMC timestamps were available offline, so the news filter (section 16 of your brief) was not tested. It needs a calendar CSV with UTC timestamps; the lab can then tag trades within +/- N minutes of releases.
  • Tick-level spread and slippage. XM's history serves bars only; the spread column exists on the last three months of M1 and on M15 back to 2022, so the per-year spread model is a median, not a per-trade value. Spread-spike and slippage filters need the telemetry above from a demo run.
  • 22 of 61 months of M1 data (Dukascopy rate limit during the session). Every year is represented; the walk-forward windows are shorter than intended.
  • Your manual rules. The strategy 'performs reasonably well when traded manually' is a claim without a trade log. Without your manual trade history (time, side, entry, exit) the discretionary edge cannot be measured or compared with the bot's trades on the same days.
  • Tick data for trailing. Management is replayed on one-minute bars with a conservative path rule; tick-level trailing in the Strategy Tester exits earlier in volatile months. Every configuration here would be somewhat worse on ticks, not better.

15. Manual trading versus the bot

What you may be doing by eye that the bot does not. Each line is a testable rule once you answer it.

  • Do you wait for the candle to close, or act mid-bar? The bot acts on the closed bar. If you act earlier, your fills are better and your signals differ from the bot's.
  • Do you skip the first flip after a sharp reversal and wait for the second? Signal age >= 10 bars is the mechanical version; it was the only replicating filter.
  • Do you skip flips that happen inside the same price zone as the last one? Distance >= 2 ATR from the previous signal is that rule.
  • Do you refuse trades when the stop would be 'too far'? The ATR cap (4 ATR) is that rule; you probably apply something like it by eye, and the bot never did.
  • Do you only trade certain hours? The data says no hour is reliably good; if you trade only London/NY, your edge is not coming from the clock.
  • Do you check M15 or H1 before entering? Say which way. Aligned trades won in 2025 to 2026 and lost in 2021 to 2024; if you switch between fading and following the higher timeframe by feel, that switch is the edge and needs a rule.
  • Do you avoid large candles or news minutes? Overextension filters removed little; the news filter is untested (missing data).
  • Do you stop after a bad run? On M3 the data says it does not matter; on M1 it does.
  • Do you take partial profits at +1R? 13% of trades that reach +1R end as losers; a partial at +1R is a reasonable rule to test with your sizing.
  • Do you ever add to a winner, or hold through an opposite flip? Holding through the flip beat every close model; adding was not tested.
  • Which trades do you not take that the bot takes? Write down the last 20 signals you skipped and why. Those reasons are the filter list that matters.
